# Decision and Experiment

1. Recover the objective, decision owner, deadline, spending limit, audience/customer, and must-haves from the latest request and current sources. Distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions.
2. Identify the actual problem, existing workaround, and credible options, including keeping the current approach where relevant. Retrieve current primary evidence for unstable claims; use research and domain specialists when needed.
3. Evaluate must-haves before weighting preferences. Do not recommend an option that fails a requirement merely because it is cheaper. Make uncertain demand and missing functions explicit.
4. Calculate material economics with consistent units and time horizons. Include supported setup, recurring costs, switching effort, and missing capabilities. Vary assumptions that could reverse the result. Never invent probabilities, returns, demand, or a cash budget.
5. Challenge the strongest assumption. Connect opportunity signals to a buyer/problem, mechanism, alternatives, timing, and invalidating evidence.
6. Recommend an option and explain the controlling tradeoff. Define a bounded test: question, observable result, owner, maximum time and incremental spend, success criterion, stop rule, and decision after each outcome. Label proposed limits unless already authorized.
7. Check that the recommendation follows the criteria and the test could change the decision. Return fewer opportunities when evidence is weak. Usually add at most one unrequested strategic next step.

Proceed with reversible steps inside existing authorization. Obtain missing authority before spending, contacting people, purchasing plans, changing the strategic objective, or creating a new ongoing responsibility. Do not ask again for authorization already granted.
